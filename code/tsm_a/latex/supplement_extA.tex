\section{Extensions I in Detail: Other Planning Approaches and Other Uncertainty}
\label{sec:S-extA}

This section gives the formulations, full results, and run records behind the article's section ``Extensions I.'' The scripts are in \texttt{code/tsm\_a/} and every output, with a \texttt{run.json} that records the command, seed, commit, solver version, and runtime, is in \texttt{results/tsm\_a/<run\_id>/} of the code repository. All runs are solver runs (PuLP~3.3.0 with CBC~2.10.13); none uses a language model.

\subsection{Common Setup}

One model builder extends the article's Eqs.~(1) to~(9) with the options below; with its default options it is the article's recourse problem, and it reproduces the Step~2 code to $9.1\times10^{-13}$ in objective value and the baseline RP, EEV, and VSS (\$6{,}380.66, \$6{,}288.50, \$92.16) exactly. Every plan fixes the wafer starts $x_f$; it is then evaluated as the article evaluates the deterministic plan, by fixing $x_f$ and re-optimizing the recourse in each scenario. Crossings are located by linear interpolation between the 50 half-ranges of Step~5 (the rule of Steps~5 and~6); onsets of the service gap by bisection to $10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Other Planning Approaches: Formulations}

Let $P_\xi$ denote the profit of scenario $\xi$: the bracket in Eq.~(1) minus $c_F\sum_f x_f$.

\emph{Static box-robust plan}~\cite{soyster1973}. Eqs.~(1) to~(9) with the single scenario $(y^F_{\text{lo}},\bar q,y^B_{\text{lo}})$, or $(y^F_{\text{lo}},q_{\text{hi}},y^B_{\text{lo}})$ for the variant with demand at its high level.

\emph{Adjustable robust plan}~\cite{bental2004adjustable}. $\max t$ subject to $t\le P_\xi$ and Eqs.~(2) to~(9) for every $\xi$, so the recourse is scenario-wise. The variant robust in the yields only maximizes $\sum_q \pi_q t_q$ subject to $t_q\le P_\xi$ for every $\xi$ with demand $q$.

\emph{Budgeted plan}~\cite{bertsimas2004}. The three yields (FE1, FE2, BE) are at their mean ($\bar y^F=(y^F_{\text{lo}}+y^F_{\text{hi}})/2$, $\bar y^B=0.80$) or at their low level, with at most $\Gamma$ of them low; each fab has its own FE yield in Eq.~(3). The worst case is taken over the vertices of this budget set, crossed with the two demand levels, with the objective of the variant robust in the yields. This is a finite-scenario version of the budget of uncertainty. Its $\Gamma=3$ case has the same worst case as the variant robust in the yields (every yield low), and its $\Gamma=0$ case ignores yield risk.

\emph{Chance-constrained plan}~\cite{charnes1959,luedtke2008sample}. Eqs.~(1) to~(9) with $m_\xi\le m^0_\xi+s+q_\xi b_\xi$ for every $\xi$, $\sum_\xi\pi_\xi b_\xi\le\epsilon$, $b_\xi\in\{0,1\}$, and $s=0$. Here $m^0_\xi$ is the unmet demand of scenario $\xi$ when FE supply is unlimited (solved: 120 units in the scenarios with high demand and low BE yield, 0 otherwise), so the constraint concerns only avoidable shortfall.

\emph{CVaR plan}~\cite{rockafellar2000}. $\max\,(1-\lambda)\sum_\xi\pi_\xi P_\xi+\lambda\big[\eta-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\sum_\xi\pi_\xi u_\xi\big]$ with $u_\xi\ge\eta-P_\xi$, $u_\xi\ge0$, $\alpha=0.9$. With eight scenarios of probability $0.125>1-\alpha$, CVaR$_{0.9}$ is the profit of the worst scenario, so $\lambda=1$ is the max-min problem and $\lambda=0$ is the RP (the latter checked at every half-range).

Table~\ref{tab:S-extA-paradigms} lists every plan at the base wafer cost and at $c_F=\$15$, and Fig.~\ref{fig:S-extA-paradigms} shows the loss of selected plans against the RP. At $c_F=\$15$ the deterministic plan's loss exceeds 2\% from $h=0.26$, and the box plans and the plan robust in the yields stay within 0.09\% of the RP. The chance constraint with $\epsilon=0$ is infeasible from $h=0.46$ and the one with $\epsilon=0.125$ at $h=0.50$, at both wafer costs; those points are omitted.

\subsection{Capacity Uncertainty: Model and Full Results}

\emph{Outsourced capacity.} Eq.~(4) uses a scenario capacity $L^G_{g\xi}\in\{(1-\delta)L^G_g,(1+\delta)L^G_g\}$, each with probability one half, independent of yields and demand.

\emph{In-house capacity.} Planned starts satisfy $x_f\le(1+\delta)L^F_f$. The wafers processed in scenario $\xi$ are $r_{f\xi}=\min(x_f,L^F_{f\xi})$ with $L^F_{f\xi}\in\{(1-\delta)L^F_f,(1+\delta)L^F_f\}$ (a common shock to both fabs), enforced by $r_{f\xi}\le x_f$, $r_{f\xi}\le L^F_{f\xi}$, $r_{f\xi}\ge x_f-\bar L_f d_{f\xi}$, $r_{f\xi}\ge L^F_{f\xi}-\bar L_f(1-d_{f\xi})$, $d_{f\xi}\in\{0,1\}$, $\bar L_f=(1+\delta)L^F_f$. Eq.~(3) uses $r_{f\xi}$ in place of $x_f$, and $c_F$ is charged on $r_{f\xi}$.

\emph{Deterministic plan.} The expected-value problem uses the mean capacities and does not determine the split of wafers between the identical fabs. We split in proportion to fab capacity. With the split returned by CBC (FE1 filled first), the deterministic plan's \%VSS is at least 2.02, 3.11, and 5.97 at every half-range for $\delta=0.1$, 0.2, and 0.3.

Table~\ref{tab:S-extA-capacity} gives every configuration; Fig.~\ref{fig:S-extA-capacity} shows the service gap under uncertain $L_G$ and \%VSS under uncertain in-house capacity.

\subsection{Scenarios from Limited Data: Method and Full Results}

\emph{Truth.} FE yield $\sim$ Beta$(a,b)$ with mean 0.875, moment-matched as in Section~\mbox{S-VI}: $(a,b)=(16.14,2.31)$ for standard deviation 0.075 and $(3.38,0.483)$ for 0.15. The optimal stochastic plan under the truth starts 14.64 and 18.00 wafers (expected profit \$6{,}376.81 and \$6{,}350.96); the deterministic plan from the true mean starts 10.29 wafers; the true VSS is \$96.79 (1.52\%) and \$164.82 (2.60\%). A Monte Carlo check with 200{,}000 draws gives \$6{,}376.78 and \$6{,}351.14 for the optimal expected profit.

\emph{Exact evaluation.} With the wafer starts fixed, the recourse value of $D$ in-house dies, $Q(D;q,y^B)$, is concave and piecewise linear in $D$. We tabulated it with CBC at $D=0,1,\dots,1{,}800$ for each demand and BE-yield pair, recovered its pieces, and checked them against direct solves at 300 random non-integer $D$ per pair (largest deviation $6.6\times10^{-5}$, CBC's numerical tolerance). The expected recourse value under the Beta truth is then a closed-form sum of incomplete beta functions, and the SAA and expected-value problems are solved exactly over total wafer starts in $[0,18]$ by enumerating breakpoints. Against direct CBC solves of the full model on nine random samples the largest deviation is $1.9\times10^{-6}$; the same code reproduces the article's baseline RP, EEV, and VSS and the seven-point Beta VSS at standard deviation 0.07 (\$92.000; \$92.0 in the Step~1 Beta file).

\emph{Plans and statistics.} For each replication (seed \texttt{SeedSequence([20261113, $\sigma$ index, $n$, $r$])}): the SAA plan~\cite{kleywegt2002}; the deterministic plan from the sample mean; $B=20$ bootstrap resamples~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}, which give the bagged plan (average wafer starts of the resample plans) and Efron's optimism correction of the in-sample VSS; the exact optimality gap; and the one-sided 95\% gap bound of~\cite{mak1999monte,bayraksan2006assessing}, mean $+\,t_{0.95,19}\,s/\sqrt{20}$ over 20 batches of size $n$, with batches drawn from the truth or as bootstrap resamples of the data. The optional Wasserstein distributionally robust plan was not run.

Table~\ref{tab:S-extA-data} gives both truths. Under the narrower truth (standard deviation 0.075, true \%VSS 1.52\%) estimation matters little: realized and in-sample VSS are within \$2 of the true VSS from five lots on, and the in-sample \%VSS wrongly exceeds 2\% in at most 4.5\% of replications. In every cell the confidence interval of the VSS optimism contains zero. In all 2{,}000 replications the realized VSS is positive. Fig.~\ref{fig:S-extA-data} shows the spread of in-sample and realized VSS.
